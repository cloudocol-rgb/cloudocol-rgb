01/Commerce platform

Replatforming a multi-region storefront

A catalogue-heavy storefront rebuilt headless, with checkout reduced to the steps customers actually complete and content the merchandising team can change without a release.

Illustrative case study. This describes how Cloudocol runs an engagement of this shape. It is not an account of a specific client project, and no client is named or implied. Published case studies will replace these pages as they are cleared for release.

01/The problem

A store that had grown by addition. Every campaign added a template, every market added a rule, and the checkout had collected six steps that nobody could justify individually.

Pages were slow on mobile, where most of the traffic was. Merchandising could not change a banner without an engineering ticket, so campaigns shipped late or not at all. Three regions ran slightly different code, which meant every fix was made three times.

02/Approach

The fastest performance work is deleting the thing that was slow.

We started with session recordings and the checkout funnel rather than the design. Two of the six steps existed for a payment method that accounted for a rounding error of orders.

From there: one component library covering all three regions, with market differences expressed as configuration rather than forked templates. Content moved into a headless CMS so the merchandising team owns the page, and the frontend renders statically with product data fetched at the edge.

03/What shipped

Four things, in this order.

A shorter checkout
Six steps to three, with address and payment on one screen and guest checkout promoted rather than buried.
One component library
Shared across every market, with region differences as configuration. Documented in Storybook and in the design file.
Editable content
Campaign pages, banners and navigation moved to a headless CMS with a preview environment, removing engineering from the campaign path.
A performance budget
Enforced in CI. A pull request that pushes the mobile bundle past the agreed limit fails before review.
